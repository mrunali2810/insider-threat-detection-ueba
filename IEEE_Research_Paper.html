Abstract—Insider threats represent the most catastrophic cybersecurity liability in modern banking and capital markets. Unlike external perimeter intrusions, malicious insiders leverage legitimate credentials, understand surveillance blind spots, and operate within trusted network zones. Conventional Data Loss Prevention (DLP) and Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) solutions fail because they rely on static volumetric thresholds that cannot detect subtle, multi-day data staging operations. Compounding this challenge, the industry-average detection-to-containment latency currently reaches 85 days, exposing financial institutions to severe fraud losses and statutory penalties.

This paper presents an end-to-end autonomous cyber-defense architecture engineered for financial infrastructure. The system integrates three synchronized layers: (1) a multi-modal User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA) profiler tracking 12 behavioral dimensions across authentication, file modifications, removable media, electronic mail, and web proxy egress; (2) a temporal sequence autoencoder (supporting Bidirectional LSTM and a vectorized zero-dependency NumPy fallback) that isolates anomalous operational cadences via reconstruction error trajectories; and (3) a collaborative multi-agent Security Operations Center (SOC) framework organized around an Observe-Orient-Decide-Act (OODA) loop. When composite risk scores exceed critical thresholds, specialized autonomous agents deliberate in real time, deploy dynamic honeypot decoy files to confirm adversarial intent, execute machine-speed zero-trust credential revocation within 142 milliseconds, and compile formal FinCEN Form 111 Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) narratives. Evaluated against CMU CERT insider threat benchmarks across 25 corporate accounts over a 30-day enterprise simulation, the platform achieved 100% scenario recall (4/4 critical ground-truth threats isolated) with a 0.8% false positive rate, compressing incident containment from months to under 1.4 seconds.

I. Introduction
A. The Operational Realities of Banking Cyber Risk

Securing financial technology infrastructure differs fundamentally from safeguarding generic enterprise networks. In commercial banks, wealth management groups, and trading desks, users possess daily authorizations to move capital, execute ledger adjustments, and access customer PII. An adversary who compromises these accounts—or a rogue employee driven by financial distress or pending resignation—does not need to deploy noisy network exploits or scan open ports. They operate in plain sight with authorized credentials.

According to global benchmarks published by the Ponemon Institute, insider-driven incidents cost financial organizations an average of $16.2 million per breach, with investigations requiring an average of 85 days to achieve full containment. In regulated financial jurisdictions governed by the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA § 501b), Sarbanes-Oxley (SOX § 404), and the SWIFT Customer Security Programme (CSP), an uncontained breach of this duration exposes institutions to severe legal, financial, and reputational damage.

B. Limitations of Legacy Security Controls

Three structural deficiencies undermine traditional enterprise defense stacks:

1) The Fallacy of Static Volumetric Thresholds: Conventional DLP policies enforce rigid tripwires, such as alerting on file transfers exceeding 2 GB. In banking environments, loan officers routinely bundle multi-gigabyte loan portfolios during quarter-end closings, whereas quantitative analysts export high-frequency tick databases. Hardcoded thresholds produce unmanageable alert fatigue or are effortlessly bypassed by attackers staging 1.9 GB over consecutive nights.

2) Blindness to Multi-Day Temporal Sequences: Insider attacks are chronological narratives rather than single-moment anomalies. An employee logging in at 2:00 AM is uncommon; downloading an SSN table is sensitive; connecting an unauthorized USB drive is suspicious. Occurring individually across separate shifts, legacy SIEM correlation rules treat each action as benign noise. However, chained sequentially over 72 hours, they constitute an unmistakable exfiltration signature.

3) The Containment Latency Gap: Detecting an ongoing breach provides little defense if the remediation decision sits in an analyst's ticket queue over a weekend. By the time a human investigator evaluates the incident, proprietary databases have already exited the organization.

C. Principal Contributions

To eliminate these vulnerabilities, this research makes four primary contributions:

• Dense 12D Behavioral Feature Extraction: Aggregation engine ingesting heterogeneous raw logs (Logon, Devices, Files, Email, Proxy, AD) conforming to CMU CERT specifications into daily 12-dimensional behavioral vectors.

• Dual-Baseline Cohort & Self Profiling: Evaluates directional positive Z-scores against both the individual's baseline and their departmental cohort, boosted by compound threat nexus multipliers.

• Temporal Sequence Autoencoding with Zero-Dependency Fallback: Sliding 5-day sequence autoencoder capable of running via PyTorch Bi-LSTM or through pure NumPy vectorized implementations for air-gapped enclaves.

• Agentic Multi-Role Deliberation & Autonomous Containment: Multi-agent SOC team operating on the OODA loop revoking credentials in 142 ms and compiling formal FinCEN Form 111 SAR narratives.

• Scenario 1 (Pre-Resignation Customer Data Exfiltration): Senior Loan Officer Alice Walker (USR_RET_04) in Retail Banking submits resignation. Over the weekend, she accesses 18 confidential loan databases, writes 3,850 MB to a SanDisk USB drive, and emails encrypted archives to ProtonMail.

• Scenario 2 (Rogue Trading & Ledger Manipulation): Trader Marcus Vance (USR_TRD_02) incurs unhedged deficits. At 2:45 AM from an unassigned backup terminal (PC-TRD-BACKUP-09), he places write locks on core_ledger_positions.db and alters Value-at-Risk limits (risk_limits_var.conf).

• Scenario 3 (Privileged System Sabotage): Lead DBA Devon Reed (USR_IT_01) on disciplinary review accesses the SWIFT gateway, wipes audit logs (db_transaction_audit.log), and stages access keys.

• Scenario 4 (Private Client Poaching via Cloud Storage): VP Sophia Chen (USR_WLT_03) in Wealth Management stages 850 MB of customer portfolios to an external file locker via multipart HTTP POST.

B. Statistical UEBA Engine with Peer Group Baselines

Dual reference distributions are maintained: Self Historical ($\mu_{\text{self}}, \sigma_{\text{self}}$) and Peer Cohort ($\mu_{\text{peer}}, \sigma_{\text{peer}}$). Directional standard scores:

$$Z_{\text{self}} = \max\left(0, \frac{x - \mu_{\text{self}}}{\sigma_{\text{self}} + \epsilon}\right), \quad Z_{\text{peer}} = \max\left(0, \frac{x - \mu_{\text{peer}}}{\sigma_{\text{peer}} + \epsilon}\right)$$
(2)
$$Z_{\text{comp}}(u, t, j) = 0.6 \cdot Z_{\text{self}} + 0.4 \cdot Z_{\text{peer}}$$
(3)

Compound nexus multipliers (Staging $\mathcal{N}_1$, Ledger Tampering $\mathcal{N}_2$, Sabotage $\mathcal{N}_3$) yield total score:

$$S_{\text{UEBA}} = \min\left(100, \sum_{j=1}^{12} w_j Z_{\text{comp}, j} + 15\mathcal{N}_1 + 18\mathcal{N}_2 + 20\mathcal{N}_3\right)$$
(4)
C. Deep Sequence Autoencoder Engine

Sequence tensor $\mathbf{X}_{u, t} \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 12}$ compresses into bottleneck $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$. Reconstruction error trajectory and risk score:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} = \frac{1}{60} \sum_{i=1}^5 \sum_{j=1}^{12} (X_{i, j} - \widehat{X}_{i, j})^2$$
(5)
$$S_{\text{DL}} = \min\left(100, \frac{100}{1 + \exp\left(-15 \cdot (\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} - 0.034)\right)}\right)$$
(6)
D. Hybrid Risk Fusion & Context
$$\text{Score}_{\text{final}} = 0.55 \cdot S_{\text{UEBA}} + 0.45 \cdot S_{\text{DL}} + \Delta_{\text{context}}$$
(7)
